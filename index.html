    5 The upload, step by step

    Do these in order. Steps 4, 5 and 7 are where most people lose their quality.

    1. Export your master at full quality

      From your editor, export 4K vertical (2160×3840) at your source frame rate, highest bitrate your editor offers, or ProRes / DNxHR if you have the disk space. This is your master — you are not uploading this file. Never upload straight out of a phone editing app.

    2. Decide what 120fps means for you

      TikTok plays back at 60fps at most. So 120fps is not "extra smoothness you keep" — it's a budget. Either conform it to 60fps (smooth real-time motion, half the frames discarded cleanly) or stretch it to slow motion (2× at 60fps, 4× at 30fps, every frame used). Deciding yourself beats letting TikTok's transcoder throw frames away at random. The question flow picks this for you.

    3. Run the recipe from step 3

      This produces the file you actually upload: correct resolution, correct frame rate, constant frame rate, BT.709 colour, 2-second keyframes, faststart enabled, high bitrate headroom so TikTok's encoder has clean data to work from.

    4. Turn on high-quality uploads in the app

      TikTok app → Profile → ☰ menu → Settings and privacy → Content preferences (older builds: Data Saver) → enable High quality uploads / Upload HD. Also turn Data saver off. This single toggle is the biggest free quality win, and it is off by default for many accounts.

    5. Upload from the web, not the phone

      Go to tiktok.com/upload on a desktop browser. The web uploader accepts much larger files and higher bitrates than the in-app picker, and it skips the phone's own pre-upload re-compression entirely.

    6. Do not touch the in-app editor

      Text, stickers, filters, transitions, speed ramps and trimming applied inside TikTok force an extra generation of encoding — on top of the one you already can't avoid. Burn everything into the file in your editor. If you need a trending sound, add only the sound.

    7. Post it once, on Wi-Fi, and don't use drafts

      Saving to drafts and reposting re-encodes again. Uploading on a weak cellular connection makes some clients fall back to a lower-quality path. Upload, caption, post.

    8. Check the result on a real device

      Give it 10–20 minutes. TikTok often serves a low-quality proxy immediately after posting and swaps in the good encode once processing finishes. Judging your upload in the first two minutes is the most common false alarm there is.

    6 Straight answers

    Can I really upload with no compression at all?

    No, and be suspicious of anything that says otherwise. Every file you send is transcoded on TikTok's servers into their own delivery ladder so it can stream to phones on bad connections. The upload is re-encoded whether you like it or not. The realistic goal is one generation of loss, from a pristine source, instead of three or four from a phone export that was already squeezed twice before TikTok saw it.

    Then does uploading 4K instead of 1080p do anything?

    Yes, usually. Viewers almost never receive a true 4K stream, but a 4K source gives the encoder more information per output pixel, so the 1080p it produces is sharper and holds up better on detailed textures — hair, grass, fabric, text. It also signals a high-quality source to TikTok's own processing. The cost is upload time. If your connection makes 4K painful, a very high bitrate 1080p export is a perfectly good second place.

    Will my 120fps stay 120fps?

    No. Feed playback tops out at 60fps, and a lot of delivery is 30fps. 120fps is worth shooting because it gives you clean slow motion and a sharper shutter, not because viewers see 120 distinct frames. Convert it deliberately — see step 2 above.

    Why does my video look fine on my phone and terrible in the feed?

    Three usual suspects, in order: high-quality uploads is switched off in settings; the clip was edited inside TikTok after upload; or you're looking at the temporary proxy encode in the first few minutes after posting.

    H.264 or H.265/HEVC?

    H.264 High profile is the safest possible input — universally accepted, never rejected, never silently mangled. HEVC can carry more detail per megabyte and is fine from the web uploader, but occasionally causes colour shifts or rejected uploads depending on how it's tagged. If something goes wrong with HEVC, re-export as H.264 before debugging anything else. This tool defaults to H.264 for that reason.

    What about the file size limit?

    The ceiling depends on your region, account and whether you're on the app or the web uploader, and TikTok changes the numbers regularly — so this tool doesn't hard-code them. The web uploader is always the most generous. If an upload is rejected for size, lower the bitrate cap in the recipe before you lower the resolution: bitrate is cheaper to give up than pixels.

    Is my video sent to a server?

    No. The analyzer and the optional converter run in your browser with the file handle only. This is a static site — there is no backend to send anything to. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the analyzer still works.
